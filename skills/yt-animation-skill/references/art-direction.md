# Art direction and visual development

Read when creating or reviewing characters, sets, lighting and cinematic coverage. Keep the signature style consistent; vary genre, palette, pace and emotional tone with the screenplay.

## Character identity and anatomy

Create a character bible before producing multiple scenes: identity ID, age range, height/build, silhouette, face reference views, skin undertone, eyes, hair, distinguishing features, wardrobe and approved asset version. Express personality through specific design and acting decisions rather than a generic generated face. Record costume, hairstyle and aging changes explicitly.

Use anatomically credible meshes with believable head/body ratios, joint placement, hands and feet. Stylization can simplify detail and shape language while preserving human structure. Child and adult proportions need their own references. Primitive spheres and cylinders can block a shot, but do not qualify as finished human characters.

Reuse the same approved mesh, materials, rig and wardrobe across shots, with documented scene-specific changes. Validate imported scale, topology, UVs, bone axes, shape keys and licenses before making an external asset a production dependency. Identity must survive changed lighting and camera angles.

Inspect faces in front, profile and three-quarter views, then at the most demanding close-up. Require coherent eyelids, brows, jaw, lips, teeth and eye placement. Test blinks, gaze changes, speech shapes and the story's strongest expressions. Preserve recognizable faces when expressive; reserve extreme distortion for explicit story requirements.

## Materials and grooming

Build skin from plausible color variation, roughness and restrained subsurface response, with detail appropriate to shot distance. Avoid uniform glossy highlights, excessive smoothing and waxlike surfaces. Inspect under neutral and production lighting so grading cannot hide a material defect.

Hair needs an intentional hairline, volume, breakup and readable strand/card structure, with brows and lashes where visible. Use an appropriate groom or hair cards rather than a shiny solid cap for finished close-ups. Clothing communicates fabric through fit, thickness, folds, seams, roughness and coherent texture scale. Resolve cloth/body intersections in action poses, not just a neutral pose.

Track asset sources and usage rights. Suitable third-party assets may improve production; do not assume permission to purchase or redistribute them. Generated reference images can guide design, but are not rigged production models or proof of consistent 3D identity.

## Environment and lighting

Design sets from the characters' lives and blocking: plausible dimensions, access, working doors, prop placement and intentional wear. Add motivated personal belongings and material variation without obscuring the action. Keep a location plan and continuity states for doors, lamps, phones, weather and time of day.

Use motivated light sources with exposure priorities, realistic contact shadows, material response, bounce and separation. Preserve readable acting and meaningful darkness. Evaluate shadow noise, light leaks, eye reflections, overexposed practicals and skin color under the actual camera. Flat studio lighting is a diagnostic view, not the channel's final look.

## Cinematography and grade

Use shot size, lens, height, eyelines and screen direction to communicate story. Match coverage across cuts and establish geography before relying on close-ups. Follow the protagonist from an external viewpoint for the channel's third-person default. Over-the-shoulder coverage is external; a camera at the character's eyes is POV. First-person narration alone does not authorize POV or peephole masks.

Compose for full-frame 16:9. Check headroom, hands, feet, gestures and key props at the beginning, middle and end of moving shots. Cinematic does not require black bars. Motivate camera movements with controlled acceleration and stable targets. Depth of field supports attention while preserving essential acting; it must not hide anatomy or set problems.

Establish consistent color management, exposure and white balance. Build shot-to-shot continuity before the final grade. Use contrast, color separation and saturation deliberately; a dark vignette cannot replace lighting. Review the actual delivery transform so skin, shadow detail and highlights survive export.
